# UX audit: phone and desktop (live site, 1 Oct 2026)

**Site:** aniket-portfolio-six-bice.vercel.app (main at PR #48)
**Visitor goal it was judged against:** an owner of a service business lands, gets what you do, believes you can do it, sees roughly what it costs, and gets in touch.
**Method:** 11 pages × 2 devices (iPhone 13 at 390 px, desktop at 1440 px). Each page scrolled through with screenshots, an automated accessibility scan (axe, WCAG 2.1 AA), tap-target and text-size measurements, and load timing. Each finding cites the heuristic it breaks. The concept sites (/concepts/*) were left out because they are other businesses' pages.

**Severity:** P0 blocks the goal · P1 hurts conversion or trust for many visitors · P2 friction or polish · P3 nice to have.

---

## What already works (keep it)

- **Fast.** Largest paint is under 1.7 s on every page on a phone, and nothing scrolls sideways at any size. *(Doherty threshold)*
- **One clear first action.** The hero has one saffron button, one quieter "Watch a build" option and a reassurance line. *(Hick, Von Restorff)*
- **Prices are shown.** "From ₹35,000", fixed price and a fixed date, plus "if it isn't live by the date in writing, you don't pay the second half". This is unusual and builds trust. *(Fogg: ability; Cialdini: commitment)*
- **Honest scarcity and response time.** "Next start slot is usually one to two weeks out" and "I reply personally within 24 hours". *(Cialdini: scarcity, done honestly; Nielsen #1: visibility of system status)*
- **Basics are clean.** One h1 per page, every image has alt text, a reduced-motion fallback exists, and the 404 page is friendly and points somewhere useful.
- **The contact page offers a choice.** WhatsApp ("Quickest"), email or a six-question form, and "What happens next" lays out the three steps. *(Nielsen #3: user control; Peak-end: the page ends on reassurance)*

## Findings

### P1-1 · The home page is about 18 phone screens long, and prices only appear around screen 14
**Where:** `/` on phones (15,100 px tall). Desktop is similar at 15,900 px.
**What happens:** after the hero and the story, six project cards follow. Each has 4 to 6 bullets plus a grey disclaimer paragraph. A visitor has to get through all six before learning what anything costs or how to start.
**Heuristics:** Miller's law and cognitive load (6 dense cards in a row), Hick's law, progressive disclosure, Fogg (ability drops as effort rises), Krug ("don't make me think": scanners leave).
**Fix:**
- On home, show 3 projects (Therapist PWA, Shared Inbox, Lead Research), each with a single result line and a "Read the case study" link. Add one "See all 6 projects" link.
- Move "Three ways in" (Websites / Software / AI with prices) straight after that shorter work block.
- Target: prices visible within about 8 phone screens.

### P1-2 · "Book a free call" opens WhatsApp, not a booking
**Where:** the main CTA in the hero on home, /websites, /software and /ai.
**What happens:** the label promises a calendar slot, but the tap opens a WhatsApp chat. People who don't use WhatsApp for business, or who are on a desktop without it, hit a dead end at the most important moment.
**Heuristics:** Nielsen #2 (match between system and the real world), Norman (signifiers and mapping: the label doesn't match the result), Jakob's law ("book" means a calendar on every other site).
**Fix (pick one):**
- (a) Rename it to **"Book a free call on WhatsApp"**. Smallest change.
- (b) Link it to a real scheduler (Cal.com or Calendly, free tier) and move WhatsApp to the secondary button.

(b) converts better with overseas, USD-paying visitors.

### P1-3 · Developer tool names are visible on /projects
**Where:** `/projects`. Each card shows chips such as "React 19", "Vite", "TanStack Router & Query", "Zustand", "Radix UI", "+11".
**What happens:** a clinic or studio owner reads a list of tools they don't recognise right next to the result they do care about ("1,200+ client records"). This breaks the site's own rule: no tool names in visible text, only in the closed developers panel.
**Heuristics:** Content design (write for the reader's vocabulary), Nielsen #2, Gerhardt-Powals (show only information the user needs).
**Fix:** move the chips into the closed "For developers" panel, as on the case-study pages, or drop them from the cards.

### P1-4 · Saffron pill labels fail the contrast check
**Where:** every light "cream" section: Prices, FAQ and About steps, the contact page's "Write it out", and more. That is 4 to 21 instances per page on /websites, /software, /ai, /about and /contact.
**What happens:** text #b8560a on #fbe6d2 at 14 px measures **3.97 : 1**. WCAG AA needs 4.5 : 1.
**Heuristics:** WCAG 2.1 SC 1.4.3 (contrast minimum).
**Fix:** use **#a04a08** (4.99 : 1) for the pill text on cream. It's one CSS token, and the dark pills are unaffected.

### P2-5 · A missing space in the contact heading: "aprice in writing"
**Where:** `/contact`, "What happens next". On a phone it reads **"From your message to / aprice in writing."**
**Cause:** the word-by-word heading animation (`fx('split')`) drops the space after a one-letter word when it lands at the start of a line. Other split headings with "a" or "I" can break the same way at some widths.
**Heuristics:** Nielsen #4 (consistency and standards); trust signals (a typo on the contact page reads as carelessness).
**Fix:** keep the space outside the word span, or use a non-breaking space after one-letter words in split headings. Then re-check all split headings at 390 px and 1440 px.

### P2-6 · The contact budget options are in rupees only
**Where:** `/contact` form, "Rough budget": Under ₹50k / ₹50k–₹2L / ₹2L+.
**What happens:** the rest of the site lets a visitor switch to USD. An overseas visitor who chose USD meets rupee bands at the last step and has to convert in their head.
**Heuristics:** Nielsen #4 (consistency), Jakob's law, Fogg (friction at the moment of action).
**Fix:** drive the budget labels from the same currency setting as the price cards. For example: Under $1,000 / $1,000–$4,000 / $4,000+, or whatever matches the USD price list.

### P2-7 · Desktop story beats spend about three screens on three sentences
**Where:** `/` on desktop, after the hero ("Every message you miss…", "What if the enquiries answered themselves?", "Here's what that looks like in real work."). The middle line is still blurred mid-transition after a normal scroll.
**What happens:** the story loop is the right idea, but each beat holds a whole empty viewport. On a trackpad a visitor can scroll past a line while it's still blurred, so the question never actually lands.
**Heuristics:** Nielsen #8 (aesthetic and minimalist design: empty space with no new information), Doherty threshold, Tognazzini (don't make users wait on the interface).
**Fix:** keep the beats but shorten each pin to about 60% of the viewport, and make the text sharp by the time a beat is 30% in. The phone version already reads well.

### P2-8 · Hero flow and demo labels are too small to read on a phone
**Where:** the home hero flow ("09:00 · 09:30 · 10:00", "Booked · Mon 5 Oct, 10:00 to 10:45"), plus the service-hero mock-ups ("Front desk · 5 tools", "aura-skin-studio.example"). That is 53 text elements under 13 px on home, many at 10 px.
**What happens:** the flow is the proof of the headline, but its key moment, the booked time, is 12 px grey mono text. People skim past it.
**Heuristics:** WCAG 1.4.4 spirit (readable text), Gerhardt-Powals (make the important information salient), and your standing rule from the calendar work (time labels must be big and high-contrast).
**Fix:** set a floor of 13 px for any text in the mock-ups that carries meaning: times, "Booked", tool names. Make the final "Booked" state the brightest thing in the flow (white, about 15 px), so the story ends on a peak. *(Peak-end rule)*

### P2-9 · Small tap targets on phones
**Where:**
- "Read the case study →" on the home cards (29 px tall)
- "← All projects" (24 px)
- "Or send a message →" in the footer (24 px)
- "Open this screen full size" on case studies (18 px)
- the footer "AI" link (14 px wide)
- the USD/INR toggle (33 px)

**Heuristics:** Fitts's law; Apple and Google guidance of at least 44 × 44 px (WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 only asks for 24 px, so most of these pass legally but feel fiddly).
**Fix:** give text links `padding-block` so the hit area is at least 44 px while the visual size stays the same. Make the footer links block-level with a min-height of 44 px.

### P3-10 · Repeated disclaimer paragraphs on home
**Where:** the home project cards, e.g. "This signer is a working demo I built myself, not a client project… The screens are from the running app."
**Note:** the honesty is a strength, so keep it. But the tag already says "Working demo · real screens", and the full paragraph repeats it on every card in small grey text.
**Heuristics:** Content design (say it once, where it matters), Miller's law.
**Fix:** keep the tag on home and move the full paragraph to the case-study page.

### P3-11 · Navigation has one redundant item
**Where:** the desktop header has the logo plus Home, Websites, Software, AI, Projects, About, Contact, "Let's talk" and a currency toggle on service pages.
**Heuristics:** Hick's law; Jakob's law (the logo already goes home).
**Fix:** drop "Home" from the desktop nav. "Contact" and "Let's talk" go to the same place, so consider keeping only the button.

### P3-12 · Small checkboxes on the contact form
**Where:** `/contact`. The radio and checkbox circles are 18 px, but each sits inside a full-width tappable row, so this is fine as long as the whole row stays clickable. **Keep it that way.**

## Not checked
- Real-device testing (iOS Safari or a low-end Android). All results are from Chromium emulation.
- Screen reader walkthrough (VoiceOver or TalkBack). Automated checks only catch some issues.
- One case-study load took 11 s once and loaded in 0.2–0.4 s on two retries, so it looks like a cold cache, not a real problem.
- Behaviour data: no analytics were looked at. Where people actually drop off on the home page would confirm or overturn P1-1.
